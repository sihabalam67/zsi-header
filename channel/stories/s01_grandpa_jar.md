# Story video #1: "Grandpa's $1,000 Test" (working title)

Family audience: simple enough for a 9-year-old, useful for parents. On YouTube it's "not made for kids", because
it's a general-audience video the whole family can watch. Length 8-9 min, 16:9, Penny narrates.
Format test: first "story" video (plan: about 3 story videos a week, the rest market/news updates).

## Real numbers (computed Nov 24, 2026 by channel/stories/s01_numbers.py)
- **Start:** $1,000 put in an S&P 500 index fund with dividends reinvested (^SP500TR, before fees and taxes)
  on Nov 25, 1996. Its value at each point:

  | Point in time | Value |
  |---|---|
  | Sep 1, 2000 (dot-com peak) | $2,123 |
  | Oct 9, 2002 (bottom) | $1,117, down 47% |
  | Oct 9, 2007 (peak) | $2,464 |
  | Mar 9, 2009 (bottom) | $1,103, down 55%: after 12 years, only $103 more than the jar |
  | Feb 19, 2020 (before COVID) | $6,934 |
  | Mar 23, 2020 (bottom) | $4,591, down 34% |
  | Oct 6, 2026 (record) | $17,660 |
  | Nov 23, 2026 (today, during the dip) | **$16,368** |

- **Cookie jar:** prices rose 2.13x (CPI Nov 1996 158.7 → Oct 2026 337.5). The $1,000 in the jar now buys
  what **$470** bought in 1996. To buy what $1,000 bought then, you need **$2,127** today.
- **The unluckiest investor:** $100 a month starting at the Oct 2007 peak, just before the biggest crash.
  - At the March 2009 bottom they had paid $1,800 and it was worth $1,067.
  - Today they have paid $23,000 and it is worth **$100,324**.

## Characters
- **Grandpa Joe:** big white mustache, suspenders, and a terrible sense of humor. He believes in the Jar Test.
- **Nickel the parrot:** Grandpa's parrot. Running gag: at every scary moment he squawks
  "DON'T CHOP THE TREE!" (and once, wrongly, "DON'T EAT THE COOKIES!").
- **Mia (the spender):** spends money the minute it arrives on the coolest gadget of each year: a virtual pet
  keychain, light-up sneakers, a giant TV. Her closet fills up with stuff.
- **Ben (the saver):** keeps cash in a cookie jar he calls "Fort Cookie". He checks it every night and loves that he
  "never loses a dollar".
- **Rosie (the planter):** puts her $1,000 in a fund that owns a slice of America's 500 biggest companies.
  In the video, the fund is a **money tree** she plants in Grandpa's yard.
- **The Inflation Mouse:** a sneaky little mouse that lives in Ben's jar and nibbles the *value* of the money
  without touching a single bill.
- **Storms:** stock market crashes. They shake leaves (money) off Rosie's tree.

## The story (8 chapters)
1. **Cold open (2026, 0:00-0:25).** Three grown-ups stand at Grandpa's door, each holding something: a giant box of
   old gadgets, a dusty cookie jar, and a photo of a huge tree. "Thirty years ago, Grandpa gave each of us $1,000.
   Today he finds out who won. One of us has $16,368. One of us has $1,000 that's secretly worth $470. And one of us
   has a closet full of junk." Penny: "Let's go back to 1996."
2. **The Jar Test (1996).** Grandpa hands out three envelopes with one rule: "Come back in 30 years." Mia buys a
   virtual pet the same afternoon. Ben names his jar. Rosie plants a tiny sapling. Nickel: "BORING!"
3. **The first storm (2000-2002).** Rosie's tree has doubled ($2,123), and Mia and Ben are jealous. Then the dot-com
   storm hits and half the leaves blow away ($1,117). Mia: "Told you, money trees are scary." Nickel's first
   "DON'T CHOP THE TREE!"
4. **The big storm (2008-2009), the cliffhanger.** The tree regrows to $2,464, then the biggest storm in 80 years.
   In March 2009, after 12 years, Rosie's tree is worth $1,103, only $103 more than Ben's jar. Ben laughs: "Twelve
   years of worrying for $103!" Rosie holds the axe. Grandpa calls: "Storms end. Trees that are chopped don't
   regrow." *Will she chop it?* (subscribe ask right after)
5. **The sneaky mouse (Ben's chapter).** Meanwhile in Fort Cookie the bills are all there, but the Inflation Mouse
   has been nibbling. A grocery cart that $1,000 filled in 1996 gets smaller every year. Simple demo for kids: same
   money, smaller cart.
6. **The weird storm (2020).** The whole world stays home and the tree drops 34% in a month ($6,934 → $4,591).
   Rosie has learned. She makes hot chocolate and waits. Nickel, proudly: "DON'T CHOP THE TREE!"
7. **Reunion day (2026).** Grandpa opens the envelopes on a big scoreboard:
   - Mia: $0 and a closet of gadgets that no longer turn on (gag: they all beep at once).
   - Ben: $1,000. Twist: it only buys what $470 did. "I never lost a dollar… but I lost half my money?!"
   - Rosie: $16,368, even during this month's dip. Did she pick stocks? Time the market? No. She didn't chop
     the tree.
8. **Mia's second chance (ending).** Mia: "Is it too late for me?" Grandpa opens a last envelope: the true story of
   the unluckiest planter, who started $100 a month at the worst possible moment (Oct 2007, right before the
   biggest storm). By March 2009 it was worth less than they'd put in. Today: $23,000 paid in, $100,324 grown. "The
   best time to plant a tree was 30 years ago. The second best time is today." Mia plants a seed. Nickel: "DON'T
   CHOP THE TREE!"

**3 lesson cards at the end** (kids can repeat them):
1. Money in a jar shrinks (the Inflation Mouse).
2. Planted money grows; storms always come. Don't chop the tree.
3. Start small, start now, keep planting.

**Comment question:** "Which grandkid are you: Mia, Ben or Rosie?"
**Share line:** "Send this to someone who still has a cookie jar."
**Short (cut from chapter 7):** Ben opens his jar and learns his $1,000 is worth $470.

## Honesty notes (in the description and once on screen)
- The index's real past returns, not a promise about the future. Storms can last a long time.
- Before fees and taxes.
- Education, not financial advice.
- Mia, Ben, Rosie and Grandpa are made-up characters. The numbers are real.
