# Board verdict: The Barber Shop (pre-built websites for barbershops)

**Vote: 3 FUND IF, 0 PASS. Average score 4.3 / 10** (Offers 4, Monopoly 4, Product 5).

A FUND IF at 4/10 is a weak yes: the board likes the sales method and dislikes the business it is attached to right now.

## Risks more than one member raised (most serious first)

1. **$10/month may not pay for the sale.** (All three.) If a person has to call, text or walk in to close each shop, one paying shop may cost more than it earns in a year. Cost to win a shop, free-to-paid conversion and churn are all unknown. → `/founder-cfo`, `/founder-consumer`
2. **No proof yet.** (All three.) No owner has seen a site, said yes or paid. Every demand number is a guess.
3. **Free substitutes and copycats.** (Offers, Monopoly, Product.) Owners already have Google profiles, Instagram and free booking-app pages (Booksy, Vagaro, Square). Pre-building sites from public data is cheap to copy, and a booking platform could give it away. → `/founder-competitors`
4. **Selling a website, not more customers.** (Offers, Monopoly.) What a barber wants is a full chair. A site at $10 reads as a commodity.
5. **Too broad.** (Product, Monopoly.) 297 categories, 8 regions and 4,937 leads before the first barbershop has paid.

Where they disagreed:
- **Product** worries most about the experience after the reveal: claiming, paying, editing hours or prices, cancelling. It also worries that a scraped wrong price or a stock photo on "your" site kills trust at the key moment.
- **Monopoly** worries most about the moat. Nothing gets stronger as shops are added.
- **Offers** thinks the price is simply wrong and should be $50–100 with a guarantee.

## Conditions (checklist for the rest of the pack)

- [ ] Narrow to barbershops in one city (for example, one North Carolina city from the 271 already built) until shops there pay and stay.
- [ ] Show sites to a set batch of owners (for example 50) and record: saw it, said yes, paid after the free month, still paying at month 3, and cost per paying shop.
- [ ] Get at least 10 paying owners before building more leads.
- [ ] Check every site by hand before an owner sees it: hours, prices, services and photos. No generated photo may pass as the shop's real photo.
- [ ] Write down the full owner journey: contact, reveal, claim, pay, edit, cancel, and what happens to the site after cancelling.
- [ ] Promise an outcome, not a website: a monthly "your site sent you X calls and Y booking clicks" report. → `/founder-offer`
- [ ] Re-price on value: build a stack (review requests, Google profile clean-up, stats report, promos) and test a higher price with a guarantee. → `/founder-pricing`, `/founder-offer`
- [ ] Prove payback: measured cost to win a shop must earn back within a reasonable window at the real price and churn. → `/founder-cfo`
- [ ] Name one feature that makes shops hard to leave, such as owning reviews, rebooking reminders or a local directory that sends real customers.
- [ ] Find out what owners already use instead and why they'd switch. → `/founder-competitors`, `/founder-consumer`

## The strongest version the board can see

This differs from what was pitched. The asset is not a $10 website. It is the **"here's your shop's finished site" reveal**, a door-opener no agency can match on cost. The stronger business uses that reveal to sell **more booked chairs** to barbershops in one city. The site is the free hook. The paid product is a monthly growth service at a real price, roughly $50–100 per the Offers lens (to be tested, not assumed): review requests, Google profile upkeep, rebooking texts and a monthly report showing calls and bookings the shop got. That service gets stickier with every month of a shop's data. Prove it with 10 paying barbers in one city, then expand by city, then by trade.
