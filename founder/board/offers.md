# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Effort and time are close to zero for the buyer.** The site is built before the owner hears about it. That is the hardest part of the value equation to win, and this business wins it.
- **Showing the owner their own finished site makes it believable.** "Here is your site, already live" is far more convincing than a mock-up or a sales deck. The owner can see what they will get.
- **The buyers are easy to find.** There are 4,937 named leads, and 271 shops already have a site built. Reaching the buyer is not the problem.
- **Each extra site costs little to make.** The engine and templates already exist, so delivering one more site should cost little. (Hosting and support cost per shop is still unconfirmed: /founder-cfo.)

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It competes on price, and the price is too low to pay for selling it.** $10/month makes it a commodity, and "free for a month" makes the price the headline. No owner feels stupid saying no to a website. The bigger danger: if a person has to call, text or walk in to close each shop, getting one paying shop may cost more than it earns in a year or more. Nobody knows the cost to acquire a shop, the conversion rate from "here's your site" to paid, or monthly churn. These are open questions for /founder-cfo (payback at $10) and /founder-consumer (conversion and what owners would pay).
2. **The outcome on offer is a website, not more customers in the chair.** What an owner really wants is a full book of appointments. A website is one step toward that, and many shops already cover it with a Google Business Profile, Instagram, or the free page that comes with their booking app (Booksy, Vagaro, Square). If those cover most of the need, the pain is mild and the market scores weak on "massive pain". Which free options owners already have, and how good they are, is an open question for /founder-competitors.
3. **There is no proof of results yet.** The brief shows no owner has seen or paid for a site, and there is no evidence that a site brings more bookings. Without a single case of "this shop got X more calls", the offer only proves that the site exists, not that it pays off. On top of that, the pitch "the barbershop where I kill the barbershop" is unclear to a buyer and names no outcome.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- When an owner sees their site, what result do you promise them: more bookings, more calls, more reviews? How will you measure it for them?
- Who closes the sale, how, and what does one closed shop cost you in time or money?
- Have you shown a site to even 10 owners? What did they say, and did anyone pay?
- What does an owner have today (a Google profile, Instagram, a booking-app page) that makes them say "I don't need this"?
- What happens to a shop's site, domain and Google ranking if they stop paying? Is that leverage, or a risk to trust?
- Could you charge $50–100/month with a guarantee (for example, more booking clicks in 60 days or the month is free) and still be the obvious choice?
- Why barbershops first, rather than the trades in your lead list that make more per job (plumbers, electricians)?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Sell the outcome, not the website.** Make bookings or calls the promise, and track them for the owner. A monthly "your site sent you X calls and Y booking clicks" report is cheap to build and makes the value plain.
2. **Build a stack and price it on value, not at $10.** Add pieces that cost little to deliver and that owners value, such as review-request links, Google profile clean-up, a monthly stats report and seasonal promos. Back the stack with a guarantee the numbers can afford (/founder-offer to build it, /founder-cfo to check the price against the cost of getting a shop).
3. **Get proof before scaling.** Get 10 paying owners from the 271 built sites, and record the conversion rate, cost per shop and churn. The board should see real numbers before more leads are built out.
4. **Choose the market on the numbers.** Run /founder-consumer on barbershop owners and on one higher-ticket trade side by side. Lead with whichever has more pain and more money.
