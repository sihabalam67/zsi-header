# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The distribution idea is the real asset.** Building the site before the first contact flips the sale. "Here is your finished site" is a much easier yes than "do you want a website?". The Monopoly lens counts distribution as half the business, and this is the most original part of the brief.
- **Economies of scale are real on the build side.** A 297-template engine, 271 live sites and 4,937 leads mean each extra site costs almost nothing to produce. A one-off agency cannot match that cost per site.
- **There is a candidate secret:** small shop owners will not build a site themselves but may accept one that already exists. If true, it is the kind of non-obvious insight this lens looks for. It is not proven yet (no owner has seen or paid, per the brief).

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No moat; a copycat can match it in month six.** Nothing here is ten times better on the product side. Scraping public data and generating a template site is now cheap, so any agency, freelancer or AI site builder can run the same pre-built pitch. The booking platforms the sites link to (Square, Vagaro, Setmore and similar) already give shops a public page, and could add a full site for free. That would wipe out the $10 product overnight. No network effect, no switching cost and no brand yet.
2. **A crowded, commodity market at a commodity price.** $10/month is a price-competition position, which this lens says destroys profit. Free substitutes (social pages, booking-app listings, free business-profile pages) are what these owners use today. How crowded it is, and what those substitutes really give the owner, is an open question for **/founder-competitors**.
3. **Distribution is unproven and may cost more than a customer is worth.** The brief does not say who contacts owners or how (cold call, text, walk-in, email). At $10/month, even a modest cost to acquire each shop could take a year or more to earn back, and churn after the free month is unknown. Cost to acquire, conversion from free to paid and churn are open questions for **/founder-consumer** (would they pay) and **/founder-cfo** (payback at $10).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the one small market you can own first: barbershops in one North Carolina city? How many shops is that, and what share can you reach in 90 days? (open question: **/founder-competitors**)
- Of the 271 sites already built, how many owners have you shown one to, how many said yes and how many paid after the free month?
- What does it cost, in hours or dollars, to turn one shown site into one paying shop? (**/founder-cfo**)
- What stops a booking platform, or a local agency using AI, from doing exactly this next quarter?
- What gets stronger as you add shops? Shared review data, a local directory, a booking or marketing layer owners would hate to leave? If the answer is nothing, this stays a commodity.
- Why now? Is it cheap generation tools (which help copycats equally) or something only you have?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Prove distribution in one small market.** Show pre-built sites to a set number of owners in one city (for example 50 barbershops in one NC city), then report the share who said yes, the share who paid after month one and the measured cost per paying shop.
2. **Name the moat path in writing.** That means one feature that builds switching costs or network effects (for example owning the booking or reviews or repeat-customer layer, or a local directory that sends shops real customers). Without it, $10 is a race to zero.
3. **Run the numbers before scaling.** **/founder-cfo** must show that the measured cost to acquire a shop pays back within a reasonable window at the real price and churn. If it does not, raise the price, bundle more, or pass.
