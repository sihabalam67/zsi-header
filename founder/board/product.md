# Product lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The first five minutes are the best part.** An owner is shown a finished site for *their own* shop before being asked for anything. That beats any pitch deck, and the owner has almost nothing to do. This is starting from the experience and working back, and it is the right instinct.
- **It is built, not just planned.** A working engine, 271 real shop sites live (hidden from search), and booking links that go to the system the shop already uses instead of replacing it. That last choice is good restraint.
- **The offer fits in one sentence:** "Here is your website, already done. Free for a month, then $10." That is simple enough.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Half of the experience has not been designed.** The product is not the website. It is the whole path: first contact, the reveal, claiming the site, paying, changing a price or hours, and what happens when the owner stops paying. Every step after "site is built" is listed under "not yet known", and no owner has seen a site. That handover between the founder's system and a busy barber is where this breaks, and right now nothing is there.
2. **No focus.** 297 categories, 212 trade profiles, 8 regions and 4,937 leads, all before one barbershop has paid. That is breadth instead of depth. The name says barbershops, the brief says plumbers and dry cleaners too, and the slogan ("kill the barbershop") muddles the message. The one thing this business must do brilliantly has not been picked.
3. **A craft risk at the moment that matters most.** The site is built from scraped public data. If the hours, prices, services or photos are wrong, or the stock photos look like someone else's shop, the owner's first look at "their" site shows an error about their own business. That hurts trust at the reveal, the one moment the whole model depends on. Keeping hundreds of sites accurate as shops change prices and hours is also ongoing work, and $10 a month may not pay for it.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What exactly happens, step by step, from the moment an owner first hears from you to the moment their card is charged? Who is on the other end if they have a question?
- When a barber raises a haircut price, how do they change the site, and how long does it take?
- What do you cut so that barbershops in one city are done brilliantly? Which 290 templates go in the drawer?
- What does a barber get here that their booking-app page or Google listing does not already give them? (Open question for /founder-competitors.)
- Do barbers want a website at all, or do they want more chairs filled? (Open question for /founder-consumer.)
- What does it cost to find, contact and convert one paying shop, and to support it each month, and does $10 cover that? (Unknown; /founder-cfo.)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Narrow to one niche in one city:** barbershops, one market, until shops there pay and stay.
2. **Design and write down the full journey** (contact, reveal, claim, pay, edit, cancel, what happens to the site after cancelling), with no step left undefined. Then run it with a small batch of real owners and watch where it breaks.
3. **Check every site by hand before anyone sees it:** hours, prices, services, photos. No generated photo should pass as the owner's real shop.
4. **Come back with real numbers:** how many owners saw a site, how many claimed it, how many paid after the free month, and how many still pay later. Until then, everything about demand is a guess.
